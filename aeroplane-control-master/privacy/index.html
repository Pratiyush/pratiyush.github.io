Summary

Aeroplane Control Master is a game for Android phones and tablets, iPhone and iPad, and it plays fully offline. While you play, it shows one ad banner at the bottom of the screen. After a crash you may choose to watch a rewarded video to continue that shift, up to three times per shift.

Ads come from Google AdMob, which collects data as described in section 2. Signing in to Google Play Games on Android, or Game Center on iPhone and iPad, for leaderboards, achievements and a cloud backup of your progress, is optional. We, the developer, run no servers and use no analytics or crash-reporting tools of our own. We receive no personal data from the game. Section 10 shows how to delete your data.

1. Data stored on your device

The game keeps these in its private storage on your device:

  • your settings (language, sound, vibration, path warnings, whether you finished the tutorial),
  • your best scores, lifetime statistics (such as shifts played, aircraft landed, time in control), your daily missions, and the badges and ranks you have earned, with the day you earned them and which ones the game has already shown you,
  • a paused shift, so you can continue it later,
  • your ad consent choices, stored by Google’s consent tool (section 2).

None of this is sent to us. It is deleted when you uninstall the game (on Android also when you clear its storage), and “Reset best scores” in Settings clears your scores at any time.

2. Advertising (Google AdMob)

While you play, the game shows one banner at the bottom of the screen. After a crash it may offer a rewarded video: you choose whether to watch it, and if you do, your shift continues, up to three times per shift. The ads are served by Google’s Mobile Ads SDK. When the game uses it, the SDK collects the following and shares it with Google:

  • your IP address, which can indicate your approximate location,
  • device identifiers: on Android the advertising ID, the app set ID and, where applicable, identifiers of accounts signed in on the device; on iPhone and iPad the identifier for vendors (IDFV), see below,
  • how you use the app and its ads, such as app launches, taps and video views,
  • diagnostic information, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy use.

Google uses this data to show and measure ads, for analytics and to prevent fraud. It is encrypted in transit. On devices that support them, the SDK may also use Android’s privacy-preserving advertising APIs (Privacy Sandbox), which you control in your device’s privacy settings.

On iPhone and iPad the game does not ask for permission to track you (Apple’s App Tracking Transparency), so the SDK never receives your device’s advertising identifier (IDFA). Instead it uses the identifier for vendors (IDFV), which your device gives only to apps from the same developer and which changes once you delete all of that developer’s apps, together with your IP address. Ad installs and clicks are measured with Apple’s SKAdNetwork, which reports them to ad networks without identifying you. The SDK also sends Google crash logs of its own.

Consent (EEA, UK and Switzerland, on Android, iPhone and iPad alike): before any ad is requested, Google’s consent form lets you accept, choose purposes and ad partners, or refuse. If you refuse, Google may still show limited ads that are not personalised and do not use your advertising ID. You can change your choice at any time under Settings › Privacy options in the game.

Everywhere: you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your device settings (on most phones Settings › Google › Ads, or Settings › Privacy › Ads). On iPhone and iPad the game has no advertising ID to reset, because it does not ask to track you; the IDFV goes when you delete the game. How Google uses information from apps that show its ads: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

Offline the game requests no ads. A rewarded video loaded earlier may still play, and Google may receive the related data once you are online again.

3. Purchases (Google Play and App Store)

The game offers no purchases. It does not connect to the billing service of Google Play or the App Store, and we receive no order records. Should a purchase be added later, this section will describe it before it appears in the game.

4. Google Play Games and Game Center (optional)

You do not need to sign in: without it the whole game works, only leaderboards, achievements and the cloud backup are missing. On Android, if Google Play Games is set to sign you in automatically, it does so when the game starts; otherwise you sign in only if you tap “Sign in with Google Play Games” in Settings. Once you are signed in, Google Play Games Services links the game to your gamer profile (player ID, gamer name and avatar), and the game sends Google:

  • your scores from normal and Daily Sky shifts, for the two leaderboards,
  • the achievements you unlock,
  • one cloud save with your best scores, lifetime statistics, badges and ranks (with their dates), which of them the game has already shown you, and whether you finished the tutorial.

Your settings and your paused shift are not uploaded. Other players can see your gamer name, scores and achievements as your Play Games profile visibility allows (Everyone, Friends only or Only you).

The game includes Google’s Play Games library, which starts when the game starts and, according to Google, sends Google technical diagnostics and usage information about the library itself, to keep it stable and improve it (https://developer.android.com/games/pgs/data-collection). All of this is encrypted in transit and stored by Google. You can delete this game’s Play Games data, or your whole Play Games profile, at https://play.google.com/games/profile or in your Google Account (https://myaccount.google.com). To stop the game from using Play Games, disconnect it in your Play Games settings.

On iPhone and iPad the game uses Apple’s Game Center instead, where it offers it, and only if you are signed in to Game Center on your device (Settings › Game Center). Then the game sends Apple your scores for the two leaderboards, the achievements you unlock and the same saved game as above, which Game Center keeps in your iCloud account if iCloud is turned on. Other players see your Game Center nickname, scores and achievements as your Game Center settings allow. Apple handles this data under its own privacy policy (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/). To stop it, sign out of Game Center in your device settings.

5. What the game does not do

It contains no analytics or crash-reporting tools of our own, you have no account with us, and we do not sell or rent personal data or build profiles of you. Personalised ads are Google’s (section 2), and in the EEA, UK and Switzerland only with your consent. The game cannot access your precise location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos or files.

If you install the game from Google Play, Google may collect crash reports and installation statistics from your device under its own privacy policy and your device settings. We see only aggregated, anonymised reports. On iPhone and iPad, Apple shares crash reports and usage statistics with app developers only if you agree to it (Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements), and we see those only in aggregated, anonymised form too.

6. Permissions

The game asks for no permission that Android would show you: no location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage or notifications. In the background it uses internet and network-state access (ads, purchases, Play Games), the advertising ID (ads) and Google Play billing (declared by the store library, unused while the game offers no purchases), plus technical permissions that Google’s libraries add for background work and Android’s ad-services APIs. Vibration uses the system’s built-in haptic feedback, which needs no permission. On iPhone and iPad the game asks for no permission either: no tracking (App Tracking Transparency), location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos or notifications.

7. Backups

You can turn it off in your device’s backup settings. On iPhone and iPad, an iCloud or computer backup of your device may likewise include that data; it is yours too, and you control it in your iCloud settings.
